Unit 5

Period 5: 1844–1877

Manifest Destiny, slavery, sectionalism, Civil War, emancipation, and Reconstruction.

Exam weighting: 10–17% 12 CED topics

Unit Overview

Manifest Destiny, slavery, sectionalism, Civil War, emancipation, and Reconstruction.

Use this page as your landing place for notes, review, and historical thinking practice. The outline below is paraphrased from the AP U.S. History CED topic structure.

Practice Focus

For this unit, prioritize these AP habits:

  • Explain developments in context.
  • Use evidence to support a claim.
  • Connect causes, effects, and continuities.

Unit Objectives

  • Explain how expansion intensified debates over slavery.
  • Analyze why political compromise broke down in the 1850s.
  • Explain how the Civil War transformed federal power and the meaning of freedom.
  • Evaluate the successes and failures of Reconstruction.
  • Practice comparison across regions, political positions, and postwar outcomes.

Topic Focus What students should be able to do
5.1 Contextualizing Period 5 Explain the context for territorial expansion, conflict, and Reconstruction.
5.2 Manifest Destiny Explain causes and effects of westward expansion.
5.3 The Mexican–American War Analyze causes and consequences of the war with Mexico.
5.4 The Compromise of 1850 Explain how compromise attempted to address sectional conflict.
5.5 Sectional Conflict: Regional Differences Analyze economic, political, and social differences between North and South.
5.6 Failure of Compromise Explain why compromise failed in the 1850s.
5.7 Election of 1860 and Secession Explain the causes and effects of Lincoln’s election and Southern secession.
5.8 Military Conflict in the Civil War Analyze major developments in the Civil War.
5.9 Government Policies During the Civil War Explain how wartime policies reshaped federal power and society.
5.10 Reconstruction Explain goals, achievements, and failures of Reconstruction.
5.11 Failure of Reconstruction Analyze why Reconstruction ended and what effects followed.
5.12 Comparison in Period 5 Compare arguments, regions, policies, and outcomes from 1844 to 1877.

CED Timeline

1846Mexican-American War begins

U.S. expansion intensified debate over slavery in newly acquired territory.

1850Compromise of 1850

Congress attempted to settle sectional disputes, but the tensions remained unresolved.

1857Dred Scott decision

The Supreme Court ruled that Congress could not ban slavery in the territories.

1861Civil War begins

Secession and war turned sectional conflict into a national crisis.

1863Emancipation Proclamation

The war became a struggle over slavery and freedom as well as union.
