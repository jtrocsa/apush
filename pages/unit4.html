Unit 4

Period 4: 1800–1848

Democracy, market revolution, reform, nationalism, expansion, and growing sectional tensions.

Exam weighting: 10–17% 14 CED topics

Unit Overview

Democracy, market revolution, reform, nationalism, expansion, and growing sectional tensions.

Use this page as your landing place for notes, review, and historical thinking practice. The outline below is paraphrased from the AP U.S. History CED topic structure.

Practice Focus

For this unit, prioritize these AP habits:

  • Explain developments in context.
  • Use evidence to support a claim.
  • Connect causes, effects, and continuities.

Unit Objectives

  • Explain how democracy expanded and remained limited.
  • Analyze the Market Revolution’s effects on regions, labor, and families.
  • Explain the goals and limits of reform movements.
  • Evaluate debates over federal power, states’ rights, and Native removal.
  • Practice causation with economic change, reform, and sectional tension.

CED-Based Topic Outline

Topic Focus What students should be able to do
4.1 Contextualizing Period 4 Explain the context for political, economic, and social change from 1800 to 1848.
4.2 The Rise of Political Parties and the Era of Jefferson Explain the development of political parties and Jeffersonian democracy.
4.3 Politics and Regional Interests Analyze how regional interests shaped politics and federal policy.
4.4 America on the World Stage Explain diplomatic and military developments in the early republic.
4.5 Market Revolution Analyze how transportation, communication, and production changed the economy.
4.6 The Growth of an American Culture Explain how art, literature, religion, and ideas shaped national culture.
4.7 Expanding Democracy Analyze the causes and effects of expanding white male suffrage and party politics.
4.8 Jackson and Federal Power Explain debates over federal power during the Jacksonian era.
4.9 The Development of an American Culture Explain how cultural movements reflected and shaped American identity.
4.10 The Second Great Awakening Analyze the causes and effects of evangelical revivalism.
4.11 An Age of Reform Explain reform movements and their goals.
4.12 African Americans in the Early Republic Analyze the experiences of enslaved and free African Americans.
4.13 The Society of the South in the Early Republic Explain how slavery shaped Southern society and economy.
4.14 Causation in Period 4 Evaluate causes and effects of major developments from 1800 to 1848.

CED Timeline

1803Louisiana Purchase

Jefferson’s expansion doubled the size of the United States and intensified debates over slavery and power.

1808Atlantic slave trade banned

Congress ended the legal importation of enslaved Africans, though slavery continued to expand domestically.

1820Missouri Compromise

Congress tried to balance free and slave states while delaying sectional conflict.

1830Indian Removal Act

Federal policy forced Native peoples westward and reshaped the nation’s expansion.

1848Seneca Falls Convention

Women’s rights activists challenged gender inequality and expanded reform politics.
