Unit 6

Period 6: 1865–1898

Industrial capitalism, immigration, urbanization, the West, labor conflict, and the Gilded Age.

Exam weighting: 10–17% 14 CED topics

Unit Overview

Industrial capitalism, immigration, urbanization, the West, labor conflict, and the Gilded Age.

Use this page as your landing place for notes, review, and historical thinking practice. The outline below is paraphrased from the AP U.S. History CED topic structure.

Practice Focus

For this unit, prioritize these AP habits:

  • Explain developments in context.
  • Use evidence to support a claim.
  • Connect causes, effects, and continuities.

Unit Objectives

  • Explain how industrialization transformed the economy and society.
  • Analyze the effects of westward expansion on Native peoples and settlers.
  • Evaluate arguments about industrial leaders as captains of industry or robber barons.
  • Explain labor, immigration, and reform movements in the Gilded Age.
  • Practice continuity and change across Reconstruction’s aftermath and industrial growth.

CED-Based Topic Outline

Topic Focus What students should be able to do
6.1 Contextualizing Period 6 Explain the context for industrialization and social change after the Civil War.
6.2 Westward Expansion: Economic Development Analyze economic motives and effects of western expansion.
6.3 Westward Expansion: Social and Cultural Development Explain how expansion affected Native peoples, settlers, and federal policy.
6.4 The New South Analyze continuity and change in the post-Reconstruction South.
6.5 Technological Innovation Explain how technology changed production, transportation, and communication.
6.6 The Rise of Industrial Capitalism Analyze business consolidation, corporations, and industrial leaders.
6.7 Labor in the Gilded Age Explain worker responses to industrialization and the causes of labor conflict.
6.8 Immigration and Migration in the Gilded Age Analyze causes and effects of migration and immigration.
6.9 Responses to Immigration Explain debates over immigration, nativism, and citizenship.
6.10 Development of the Middle Class Analyze social and cultural changes tied to industrial growth.
6.11 Reform in the Gilded Age Explain reform responses to industrialization and inequality.
6.12 Controversies over the Role of Government in the Gilded Age Analyze debates over regulation, money, tariffs, and government power.
6.13 Politics in the Gilded Age Explain political patterns, party competition, and reform efforts.
6.14 Continuity and Change in Period 6 Evaluate continuities and changes from 1865 to 1898.

CED Timeline

186513th Amendment

Slavery was abolished in the United States, though freedom was constrained by new forms of inequality.

1869Transcontinental Railroad completed

Railroad expansion linked the nation economically and accelerated western settlement.

1877Reconstruction ends

Federal support for Black political rights weakened as southern Democrats regained power.

1882Chinese Exclusion Act

Immigration policy reflected growing nativism and labor conflict in the Gilded Age.

1898Spanish-American War

U.S. imperial expansion overseas marked a new era in foreign policy.
