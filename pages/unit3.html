Unit 3

Period 3: 1754–1800

Imperial crisis, revolution, founding documents, republican government, and the early republic.

Exam weighting: 10–17% 13 CED topics

Unit Overview

Imperial crisis, revolution, founding documents, republican government, and the early republic.

Use this page as your landing place for notes, review, and historical thinking practice. The outline below is paraphrased from the AP U.S. History CED topic structure.

Practice Focus

For this unit, prioritize these AP habits:

  • Explain developments in context.
  • Use evidence to support a claim.
  • Connect causes, effects, and continuities.

Unit Objectives

  • Explain how the Seven Years’ War led to imperial crisis.
  • Analyze arguments for and against independence.
  • Explain how revolutionary ideals expanded and remained limited.
  • Evaluate the strengths and weaknesses of the Articles and Constitution.
  • Practice continuity and change across the revolutionary era.

CED-Based Topic Outline

Topic Focus What students should be able to do
3.1 Contextualizing Period 3 Explain the historical context of the American Revolution and the early republic.
3.2 The Seven Years’ War Explain how imperial conflict changed relationships between Britain, colonists, and Native peoples.
3.3 Taxation Without Representation Analyze causes of colonial resistance to British policies.
3.4 Philosophical Foundations of the American Revolution Explain how Enlightenment and republican ideas shaped revolutionary arguments.
3.5 The American Revolution Explain causes and effects of the war for independence.
3.6 The Influence of Revolutionary Ideals Analyze how revolutionary ideas affected different groups in American society.
3.7 The Articles of Confederation Explain strengths and weaknesses of the first national government.
3.8 The Constitutional Convention and Debates over Ratification Analyze the compromises and debates that shaped the Constitution.
3.9 The Constitution Explain how the Constitution structured power and addressed problems of the Articles.
3.10 Shaping a New Republic Analyze political, economic, and diplomatic developments in the 1790s.
3.11 Developing an American Identity Explain competing ideas about national identity in the early republic.
3.12 Movement in the Early Republic Explain migration, settlement, and interactions with Native peoples.
3.13 Continuity and Change in Period 3 Evaluate what changed and what continued from 1754 to 1800.
