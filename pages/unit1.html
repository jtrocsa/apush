Period 1: 1491–1607

Native societies, European contact, the Columbian Exchange, and early Spanish colonization.

Exam weighting: 4–6% 7 CED topics

Unit Overview

Native societies, European contact, the Columbian Exchange, and early Spanish colonization.

Use this page as your landing place for notes, review, and historical thinking practice. The outline below is paraphrased from the AP U.S. History CED topic structure.

Practice Focus

For this unit, prioritize these AP habits:

  • Explain developments in context.
  • Use evidence to support a claim.
  • Connect causes, effects, and continuities.

Unit Objectives

  • Explain how geography shaped Native societies before European contact.
  • Analyze the causes of European exploration and colonization.
  • Explain the effects of the Columbian Exchange on the Americas, Europe, and Africa.
  • Evaluate how Spanish colonization changed labor systems, religion, and social structures.
  • Practice causation by connecting contact, conquest, disease, and demographic change.

CED-Based Topic Outline

Topic Focus What students should be able to do
1.1 Contextualizing Period 1 Describe the major societies and regional patterns in the Americas before sustained European contact.
1.2 Native American Societies Before European Contact Explain how geography shaped diverse Native American societies across North America.
1.3 European Exploration in the Americas Explain the causes and effects of European exploration and conquest.
1.4 Columbian Exchange, Spanish Exploration, and Conquest Analyze how the exchange of plants, animals, diseases, people, and ideas transformed both hemispheres.
1.5 Labor, Slavery, and Caste in the Spanish Colonial System Explain how Spanish colonization reshaped labor systems and social hierarchies.
1.6 Cultural Interactions Between Europeans, Native Americans, and Africans Analyze how different groups adapted to, resisted, and transformed one another through contact.
1.7 Causation in Period 1 Evaluate causes and effects of transatlantic contact and colonization.
