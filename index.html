DBQ Help

DBQ Formula

A strong DBQ does not just summarize documents. It uses evidence to support a historical argument.

  1. Answer the prompt with a defensible thesis.
  2. Set up the historical context.
  3. Use documents as evidence, not decoration.
  4. Explain sourcing through HIPP.
  5. Add outside evidence.
  6. Connect your argument to a broader historical development.

“Documents are clues. Your job is to turn clues into an argument.”

HIPP Practice

Use HIPP to explain why a source says what it says.

H — Historical Situation

What was happening at the time that shaped this source?

I — Intended Audience

Who was this source created for, and how does that matter?

P — Purpose

What was the author trying to accomplish?

P — Point of View

How did the author’s position, identity, or beliefs shape the source?
